CHAPTER 1 — HOW STOCK MARKETS WORK
=====================================

1.1 What is a Stock?
A stock (also called a share or equity) represents partial ownership in
a company. When a company needs to raise capital, it may sell shares to
the public through an Initial Public Offering (IPO). After that, shares
trade on stock exchanges like NYSE (New York Stock Exchange) or NASDAQ.

Example:
  TechNova Corporation has 350 million shares outstanding.
  If you own 350 shares, you own 0.0001% of the company.
  If TNOV stock is at $220/share, those 350 shares are worth $77,000.

1.2 How Prices Are Set
Stock prices are determined by supply and demand:
  - More buyers than sellers → price rises
  - More sellers than buyers → price falls

Major price-moving factors:
  - Earnings reports (quarterly results)
  - Revenue growth expectations
  - Macro factors (interest rates, inflation, GDP growth)
  - Industry news and competition
  - Regulatory decisions
  - Management changes
  - Mergers & acquisitions

1.3 Major U.S. Stock Exchanges
  NYSE (New York Stock Exchange):
    - Oldest U.S. exchange (founded 1792)
    - Home to large, established companies (GE, JPMorgan, GreenFin)
    - Physical trading floor in lower Manhattan + electronic trading

  NASDAQ:
    - Electronic exchange, founded 1971
    - Technology-focused (Apple, Microsoft, TechNova)
    - Faster electronic execution

  S&P 500 Index:
    - Tracks 500 largest U.S. public companies by market cap
    - Widely used as a benchmark for portfolio performance
    - If S&P 500 is up 10%, fund managers compare their own returns to this

1.4 Market Hours
  Regular Trading:    9:30 AM - 4:00 PM ET (Monday-Friday)
  Pre-Market:         4:00 AM - 9:30 AM ET
  After-Hours:        4:00 PM - 8:00 PM ET
  Note: Major earnings are often released after market close to allow
  investors time to digest information before next day's open.


CHAPTER 2 — KEY FINANCIAL METRICS & RATIOS
===========================================

2.1 Revenue & Profitability
  Revenue (Sales):     Total money earned from selling products/services.
  Gross Profit:        Revenue minus Cost of Goods Sold (COGS).
  Gross Margin:        Gross Profit ÷ Revenue. Shows production efficiency.
  EBIT:                Earnings Before Interest and Taxes.
  EBITDA:              EBIT + Depreciation + Amortization. Common valuation metric.
  Net Income:          "Bottom line" — profit after all expenses and taxes.
  EPS (Earnings/Share): Net Income ÷ Shares Outstanding.

Example (TechNova Q4 2024):
  Revenue:        $4,820M
  Gross Profit:   $2,892M
  Gross Margin:   60.0%  (meaning $0.60 of every $1 revenue is gross profit)
  Net Income:     $1,214M
  EPS (diluted):  $3.47

2.2 Valuation Ratios
  P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings):
    Formula:   Stock Price ÷ Annual EPS
    Meaning:   How much investors pay per $1 of earnings.
    Example:   TNOV at $220 / $13.47 annual EPS = 16.3x P/E
    Context:   S&P 500 historical average P/E ≈ 15-20x.

  P/S Ratio (Price-to-Sales):
    Formula:   Market Cap ÷ Annual Revenue
    Meaning:   How much investors pay per $1 of revenue.
    Use:       Useful for unprofitable companies with strong revenue growth.

  EV/EBITDA:
    Formula:   Enterprise Value ÷ EBITDA
    Meaning:   Holistic valuation including debt; popular in M&A.
    Example:   TNOV EV/EBITDA 2024: 18.2x

  PEG Ratio (P/E to Growth):
    Formula:   P/E ÷ EPS Growth Rate
    Rule of thumb: PEG < 1.0 may indicate undervaluation vs growth.

2.3 Balance Sheet Metrics
  Cash & Equivalents:  Immediately available liquid assets.
  Total Debt:          All short and long-term borrowings.
  Net Debt:            Total Debt minus Cash. Negative = net cash.
  Debt-to-Equity:      Total Debt ÷ Shareholders Equity. Lower is safer.
  Book Value/Share:    Total Equity ÷ Shares Outstanding.

2.4 Cash Flow Metrics
  Operating Cash Flow:   Cash generated from core business operations.
  Capital Expenditures:  Money spent on long-term assets (buildings, equipment).
  Free Cash Flow (FCF):  Operating Cash Flow minus CapEx. The "real" earnings.
  FCF Yield:             FCF per share ÷ Stock Price. Higher = better value.


CHAPTER 3 — SEC FILINGS EXPLAINED
====================================

All U.S. public companies must file reports with the SEC. As an Atlas
Capital employee, you may encounter these documents regularly.

3.1 Form 10-K (Annual Report)
  Filed:      Annually, within 60-90 days of fiscal year-end.
  Contains:   Full-year financial statements, MD&A (Management Discussion),
              business overview, risk factors, legal proceedings.
  Example:    GreenFin Banking filed its 10-K on Feb 28, 2025 for FY2024.
  Why it matters: Most comprehensive view of a company's health and risks.

3.2 Form 10-Q (Quarterly Report)
  Filed:      Three times per year (Q1, Q2, Q3). Q4 is covered by 10-K.
  Contains:   Quarterly financial statements, key updates since last filing.
  Turnaround: Filed within 40-45 days of quarter end.

3.3 Form 8-K (Current Report — Material Events)
  Filed:      Within 4 business days of a material event.
  Triggers:   M&A deals, CEO/CFO changes, bankruptcy, major contracts,
              earnings releases, regulatory actions.
  Example:    TechNova filed 8-K on Feb 18, 2025 for SecureLayer acquisition.

3.4 Form 4 (Insider Trading Report)
  Filed:      Within 2 business days of an insider transaction.
  Who files:  Directors, officers (CEO, CFO, etc.), and 10%+ shareholders.
  What's reported: Purchases, sales, grants (RSUs, PSUs, stock options).
  Why it matters: Open-market insider PURCHASES are bullish signals.
  Example:    TechNova CEO's annual RSU grant reported in January 2025.

3.5 DEF 14A (Proxy Statement)
  Filed:      Before annual shareholder meeting.
  Contains:   Executive compensation, director nominations, shareholder votes,
              governance policies.
  Key metric: CEO pay ratio vs. median employee salary.

3.6 S-1 (Registration Statement for IPO)
  Filed:      Before a company goes public (Initial Public Offering).
  Contains:   Full business description, financial history, risk factors.


CHAPTER 4 — INSIDER TRADING RULES FOR EMPLOYEES
=================================================

IMPORTANT: This section applies to ALL Atlas Capital employees.
Violations can result in termination, civil penalties, and criminal charges.

4.1 What is Insider Trading?
Insider trading is buying or selling a publicly traded security while
in possession of material non-public information (MNPI) about that security.

Material Information: Information that a reasonable investor would consider
  important in making an investment decision.
  Examples: Unreleased earnings, M&A plans, regulatory decisions,
            product launch results, major contract wins/losses.

Non-Public: Information not yet disclosed to the general public through
  SEC filings, press releases, or major news outlets.

4.2 Atlas Capital Policies
  RESTRICTED LIST:
    Companies on our restricted list cannot be traded by employees in
    personal accounts. A company is added when we have MNPI about it.
    Current restricted list is maintained by Compliance (not in this doc).

  PERSONAL TRADING:
    - All personal trades in public securities must be pre-cleared by
      Compliance at least 24 hours in advance.
    - 60-day holding period applies to all personal trades.
    - Trades must be reported to Compliance within 10 days.

  TIPPING:
    Sharing MNPI with friends or family who then trade on it is also illegal.
    "Tips" carry the same legal penalty as direct insider trading.

4.3 Safe Harbors
  Rule 10b5-1 Plans:
    A pre-arranged automatic trading plan set up when you have no MNPI.
    Once established, trades execute mechanically — you have no discretion.
    This protects against insider trading accusations even if you later
    receive MNPI.
    Example: TechNova CTO's share sales were under a 10b5-1 plan.

  Blackout Periods:
    Atlas Capital enforces trading blackouts typically:
    - 2 weeks before quarter-end through 48 hours after earnings release.
    Check with Compliance for current blackout dates.

4.4 Penalties
  Civil:     SEC can seek disgorgement of profits + penalty up to 3x profits.
  Criminal:  Up to 20 years imprisonment and $5 million fine (individuals).
  Firm:      Up to $25 million fine per violation.

IF IN DOUBT: Do not trade. Contact Compliance immediately.


CHAPTER 5 — GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS
====================================

Alpha:          Portfolio return above the benchmark. Positive alpha = outperformance.
AUM:            Assets Under Management. Total value of investments managed.
Bear Market:    Market decline of 20%+ from recent highs.
Beta:           Measure of volatility vs benchmark. Beta > 1 = more volatile.
Bull Market:    Market rise of 20%+ from recent lows. Optimistic trend.
CAGR:           Compound Annual Growth Rate. Smoothed yearly growth rate.
CET1:           Common Equity Tier 1. Core capital ratio for banks.
Drawdown:       Peak-to-trough decline in portfolio value.
Dividend:       Cash payment to shareholders from company profits.
ESG:            Environmental, Social, Governance. Non-financial risk factors.
ETF:            Exchange-Traded Fund. Basket of securities trading on exchange.
FCF:            Free Cash Flow. Cash available after capital expenditures.
Hedge:          Position designed to reduce risk of adverse price movement.
IPO:            Initial Public Offering. First sale of shares to the public.
LCR:            Liquidity Coverage Ratio. Bank metric for short-term liquidity.
Long:           Owning an asset, expecting price to rise.
Market Cap:     Total market value of outstanding shares.
MNPI:           Material Non-Public Information. Core concept in insider trading.
NII:            Net Interest Income. Banks' interest earned minus interest paid.
NPL:            Non-Performing Loan. Loan in danger of default.
P/E Ratio:      Price-to-Earnings. Valuation multiple.
RSU:            Restricted Stock Unit. Equity compensation that vests over time.
PSU:            Performance Stock Unit. Equity comp tied to performance metrics.
SEC:            Securities and Exchange Commission. U.S. markets regulator.
Short:          Borrowing and selling an asset, betting price will fall.
Sharpe Ratio:   Return per unit of risk. Higher = better risk-adjusted returns.
VaR:            Value at Risk. Maximum expected loss at a confidence level.
YoY:            Year-over-Year. Comparison to same period last year.
YTD:            Year-to-Date. Performance from January 1 to current date.
